Mooncake

Mooncakes, or yuèbǐng, are the defining food of the Mid-Autumn Festival, a holiday that falls on the fifteenth day of the eighth lunar month when the moon is at its fullest. Families gather to share tea and thin wedges of mooncake while admiring the moon, a custom said to date back to Tang-dynasty court gatherings and later tied to a legend about the moon goddess Chang'e. Their round shape symbolizes reunion and completeness, which is why mooncakes are traditionally given as gifts between relatives and colleagues before the festival. The classic Cantonese mooncake has a thin, golden-brown crust wrapped around a dense lotus seed or red bean paste, often studded with one or more salted duck egg yolks representing the moon itself. The crust gets its distinctive sheen and softening texture from golden syrup and alkaline water, which mellow over a day or two of resting after baking. Countless regional and modern variations exist, from flaky Suzhou-style pastry to snow-skin mooncakes served chilled, but the golden lotus-paste version remains the standard against which all others are measured.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 200g lotus seed paste (or red bean paste)
  • 4 salted duck egg yolks
  • 150g all-purpose flour
  • 100g golden syrup
  • 30ml vegetable oil
  • 1/4 tsp alkaline water (kansui)
  • 1 egg yolk, for glazing
  • 1 tbsp water, for glazing
  • A little flour, for dusting

How to make it

  1. 1Rinse the salted egg yolks, brush them with a little oil, and bake at 180°C for 8 minutes to firm them up.
  2. 2Mix the golden syrup, oil, and alkaline water in a bowl, then stir in the flour to form a soft, slightly sticky dough.
  3. 3Cover the dough and let it rest for at least an hour so the gluten relaxes.
  4. 4Divide the dough and the lotus seed paste into equal portions, then flatten a portion of paste and wrap it around an egg yolk.
  5. 5Roll out a piece of dough, wrap it around the filled paste, and press the whole ball into a mooncake mold to stamp the pattern.
  6. 6Place the mooncakes on a lined tray and bake at 180°C for 5 minutes to set the shape.
  7. 7Brush the surface with egg wash and return to the oven for another 12-15 minutes, until golden brown.
