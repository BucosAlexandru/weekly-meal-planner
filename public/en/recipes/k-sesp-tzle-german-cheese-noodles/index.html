Käsespätzle (German Cheese Noodles)

Spätzle is considered the signature dish of Swabia, in southwestern Germany, where locals are often called "Spätzlesschwaben" — the Swabians of spätzle — proof of regional pride in this homemade noodle. The name derives from the dialect word for "little sparrow," a playful reference to the small, irregular shape of the dough pieces. Unlike most noodles, spätzle isn't rolled and cut with a knife, but scraped or pressed directly into boiling water, a technique that guarantees a dense, slightly chewy texture, very different from Italian pasta. Käsespätzle, the cheese variant, is considered the Sunday or festive version, while plain spätzle, as a side dish, appears at everyday meals alongside roasts or stews with rich gravy.

Egg dough

A soft, elastic dough of flour, eggs and a little milk, without yeast, forms the base of the noodles.

Shaped by scraping

The dough is scraped directly into boiling water from a special board, forming irregular little pieces.

🧀

Layers of cheese

The boiled noodles are layered with grated Emmental cheese, which melts evenly throughout.

🧅

Caramelized onions

Crispy fried onions, slowly caramelized, are generously scattered on top.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 400g flour
  • 4 large eggs
  • 150ml milk
  • 1 tsp salt
  • A pinch of nutmeg
  • 300g Emmental cheese, grated
  • 3 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • Chopped parsley, for serving

How to make it

  1. 1PREPARE THE DOUGH: Mix the flour with salt and nutmeg in a large bowl.
  2. 2ADD THE LIQUIDS: Crack the eggs into the middle of the flour, gradually add the milk, stirring vigorously with a wooden spoon, until you get a smooth, elastic dough that bubbles when beaten.
  3. 3LET IT REST: Cover the dough and let it rest 20-30 minutes at room temperature, so the gluten relaxes.
  4. 4BOIL THE WATER: Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil.
  5. 5SHAPE THE SPÄTZLE: Place a portion of dough on a wet cutting board or in a special spätzle press, then scrape thin strips directly into the boiling water.
  6. 6BOIL: Boil the spätzle 2-3 minutes, until they float to the surface, then scoop them out with a slotted spoon into a bowl of cold water.
  7. 7REPEAT: Repeat the process with the rest of the dough, in batches.
  8. 8SAUTÉ THE ONION: In a large pan, melt the butter, sauté the sliced onion over low heat 15-20 minutes, stirring often, until caramelized.
  9. 9ASSEMBLE: In a large pan or pot, alternate layers of drained spätzle with grated cheese, over low heat, stirring gently, until the cheese fully melts.
  10. 10SERVE: Divide into warm bowls, sprinkle the caramelized onion on top and garnish with chopped parsley.
