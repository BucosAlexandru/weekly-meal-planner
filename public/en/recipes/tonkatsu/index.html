Tonkatsu

Tonkatsu emerged in the late 19th century, during the Meiji era, when Japan began adopting Western culinary techniques, adapting the European breaded cutlet — like the Wiener schnitzel — to local ingredients and habits. The restaurant that gave it its current form replaced the lightly oiled pan used for European cutlets with deep-oil frying, a technique already familiar from tempura cooking, producing a much thicker, crunchier crust. Around the same time, the dish shifted from being eaten with a fork and knife to being sliced in the kitchen and eaten with chopsticks, like traditional Japanese dishes. What separates a successful tonkatsu from a mediocre one comes down to a few technical details: panko breadcrumbs, with their large, airy flakes, absorb less oil than fine European breadcrumbs and stay crunchy longer; scoring the fat edge prevents the cutlet from curling into a bulge on contact with the hot oil; and moderate frying temperature, not very high, lets the meat cook through completely before the crust burns.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 4 boneless pork loin cutlets (150g each)
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • 60g flour
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 150g panko breadcrumbs
  • Oil for frying (3-4cm depth)
  • 1/4 white cabbage, finely shredded
  • Tonkatsu sauce, to serve

How to make it

  1. 1PREPARE THE CUTLETS: lightly pound the cutlets with the flat side of a meat mallet until slightly thinner, then score the fat edge in 2-3 spots so they don't curl while frying.
  2. 2SEASON: sprinkle salt and pepper on both sides.
  3. 3BREAD THEM: dredge each cutlet in flour, shaking off the excess, then in the beaten egg, then press firmly into the panko breadcrumbs so a thick, even layer sticks.
  4. 4HEAT THE OIL: pour oil into a deep pan to about 3-4cm and heat to 170-175°C — test with a pinch of panko, which should sizzle immediately.
  5. 5FRY: fry the cutlets 4-5 minutes per side, turning only once, until the crust is deep golden and the meat is fully cooked.
  6. 6DRAIN AND REST: transfer to paper towels and let rest 2-3 minutes before cutting, so the juices redistribute.
  7. 7SLICE AND SERVE: cut each cutlet into 1.5-2cm strips, serve over the shredded cabbage, with tonkatsu sauce on top or on the side.
