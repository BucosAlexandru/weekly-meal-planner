Königsberger Klopse

Königsberger Klopse take their name from Königsberg, the East Prussian capital that is now Kaliningrad in Russia, where the dish was already a fixture of city kitchens by the eighteenth century. Made from finely ground veal or a veal-pork mix bound with soaked bread and egg, the meatballs were prized for being poached rather than fried, a lighter technique associated with genteel East Prussian cooking rather than the heartier roasted fare found elsewhere in Germany. After 1945, the expulsion of Germans from East Prussia scattered the recipe across West Germany, where it became one of the most enduring dishes carried west by refugee families and, over time, a nationwide comfort-food classic despite its bittersweet history. The dish is defined by its sauce: a butter-and-flour roux loosened with the poaching broth and finished with capers, lemon, and a touch of cream, giving the meatballs a tangy, savory coating that is traditionally ladled over boiled potatoes and sometimes beets.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500g mixed ground veal and pork
  • 1 stale bread roll, soaked in milk
  • 1 egg
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 anchovy fillet, minced (optional but traditional)
  • 1 liter beef or vegetable broth
  • 40g butter and 40g flour, for the roux
  • 3 tbsp capers, plus 2 tbsp caper brine
  • 1 egg yolk, a splash of cream, lemon juice, salt, and white pepper

How to make it

  1. 1Squeeze the soaked bread roll dry and mix it with the ground meat, egg, chopped onion, anchovy, salt, and pepper until smooth.
  2. 2Shape the mixture into 12 even meatballs with wet hands so the surface stays smooth.
  3. 3Bring the broth to a gentle simmer in a wide pot, then lower the meatballs in and poach them for about 15 minutes without letting the liquid boil.
  4. 4Lift the meatballs out with a slotted spoon and set them aside, keeping the poaching broth for the sauce.
  5. 5Melt the butter in a separate pot, stir in the flour, and cook briefly before whisking in the reserved broth to make a smooth sauce.
  6. 6Simmer the sauce for a few minutes, then stir in the capers, caper brine, and a little lemon juice to taste.
  7. 7Off the heat, whisk the egg yolk with the cream and stir it into the sauce to thicken it, return the meatballs to warm through, and serve with boiled potatoes.
