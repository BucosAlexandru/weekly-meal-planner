Syrian Ma'amoul

Ma'amoul are semolina cookies traditionally prepared across Syria and the wider Levant for religious holidays, particularly Eid and Easter, when families gather to shape hundreds of them together in a single sitting. The carved wooden molds used to shape the cookies are often passed down through generations, each family's pattern distinct enough that neighbors could once recognize whose kitchen a batch had come from. Fillings vary by occasion and household, with dates being the most common, though some cooks fill theirs with walnuts or pistachios instead, and each filling traditionally gets its own distinct mold shape so the cookies can be told apart before biting into them. The dough itself takes patience: semolina needs hours to properly absorb the butter, and rushing this step results in cookies that crumble apart instead of holding their shape.

The semolina is soaked in butter for hours before the dough comes together, which is what gives ma'amoul its crumbly, melt-in-the-mouth texture.

🌴

Dates cooked into a smooth paste

Chopped dates are simmered with cinnamon and orange blossom water until they turn into a thick, spreadable filling.

🪵

Shaped with a carved wooden mold

A traditional mold presses a decorative ridged pattern into each cookie, a design that once identified which family or shape held which filling.

❄️

Finished with a snowy dusting of sugar

Once fully cooled, the cookies are generously coated in powdered sugar, a step done only after baking so the sugar stays bright white.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500g fine semolina
  • 150g unsalted butter, melted
  • 60ml warm milk
  • 1 tsp active dry yeast
  • 300g pitted dates, chopped
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 2 tbsp orange blossom water
  • Powdered sugar, for dusting
  • 50g chopped walnuts or pistachios (for filling variation)

How to make it

  1. 1Combine the semolina with the melted butter in a bowl and let it rest for a few hours, or overnight, so the semolina absorbs the fat.
  2. 2Dissolve the yeast in the warm milk and let it sit until foamy, then knead it into the semolina mixture along with a splash of water to form a soft dough.
  3. 3Cook the chopped dates with the cinnamon and orange blossom water in a small pan over low heat, stirring until they form a smooth, thick paste.
  4. 4Let the date paste cool completely before shaping it into small balls.
  5. 5Take a piece of dough, flatten it in your palm, place a ball of date paste in the center, and fold the dough around it to seal.
  6. 6Press the filled dough into a wooden mold, or shape it by hand, to create the traditional ridged pattern, then release it gently.
  7. 7Bake the cookies in a preheated oven until the bottoms are lightly golden, then let them cool completely before dusting generously with powdered sugar.
