Achichuk

Achichuk is the summer salad that appears on every Uzbek table when tomatoes reach peak ripeness, a dish so simple it can hardly be called a recipe, more a method of letting fresh ingredients speak for themselves. It is often served alongside plov, kebab, or any meat-rich dish, balancing rich meals with tangy freshness. The name achichuk comes from a word suggesting a pleasant sourness, and the recipe's simplicity actually hides great attention to ingredient quality: the tomatoes must be perfectly ripe and the onion freshly harvested. In the markets of Tashkent and Samarkand, vegetable sellers know exactly which tomatoes are right for achichuk, a sign of Uzbek respect for seasonal produce.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 4 large, very ripe tomatoes
  • 1 large red onion
  • 1 green hot pepper
  • 2 tbsp sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • Cold water for soaking the onion
  • 1 bunch chopped parsley
  • 1 bunch chopped dill
  • Ground black pepper to taste

How to make it

  1. 1Peel the red onion and slice it very thinly, then place it in a bowl of cold water with a little salt, letting it soak for 15 minutes to temper its sharpness.
  2. 2Wash the tomatoes and slice them thinly, about 5mm thick, then arrange them on a large platter, slightly overlapping.
  3. 3Cut the hot pepper into thin rounds, removing the seeds if a milder dish is preferred.
  4. 4Drain the onion well and place it over the tomatoes, along with the pepper rounds.
  5. 5Sprinkle salt and ground black pepper directly over the salad, letting it sit for a few minutes to release the tomatoes' natural juice.
  6. 6Drizzle with sunflower oil, distributing it evenly over the whole surface.
  7. 7Sprinkle the chopped parsley and dill on top just before serving, to keep the herbs' freshness.
