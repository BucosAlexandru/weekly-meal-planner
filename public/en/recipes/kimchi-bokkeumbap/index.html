Kimchi Bokkeumbap

Kimchi bokkeumbap grew out of Korean kitchens' universal instinct not to waste anything: kimchi that has gone too sour to eat raw, and rice left over from the night before, come together into one of South Korea's most beloved dishes — simple, fast, and better than the sum of its parts. The secret lies in two often-overlooked details: the rice must be cold and a day old, so the grains stay separate in the pan instead of turning to mush, and the kimchi must be well fermented, not fresh — the sourer it is, the more depth it gives when fried. Many kitchens add spam or smoked bacon for fat, and the fried egg with its runny yolk on top isn't optional in most Koreans' minds — it's how every diner customizes their own bowl right at the table.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 3 tablespoons vegetable oil, divided
  • 150 g smoked bacon or spam, diced (optional)
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 300 g well-fermented kimchi, chopped, plus 60 ml kimchi brine
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 500 g day-old cooked white rice, cold, grains separated
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 2 eggs
  • 2 sheets roasted seaweed (nori/gim), cut into thin strips
  • 2 spring onions, sliced

How to make it

  1. 1COOK THE MEAT: heat 2 tablespoons oil in a large skillet or wok over medium heat, add the smoked bacon or spam and fry 3-4 minutes until lightly browned, then set aside.
  2. 2SAUTÉ THE AROMATICS: in the same pan, add the onion and garlic, cook 2 minutes until softened, then add the chopped kimchi and stir-fry over high heat for 3 minutes — this is the step that draws out the fermented sourness.
  3. 3BUILD THE SAUCE: pour in the kimchi brine, soy sauce and sugar, stir and let it bubble gently for 1 minute to reduce slightly.
  4. 4ADD THE RICE: tip in the cold rice, breaking up any clumps with the back of a spoon, then stir-fry vigorously over high heat for 4-5 minutes, pressing the grains against the pan so each one picks up a little colour and flavour.
  5. 5FINISH: return the meat to the pan, drizzle in the sesame oil, toss briefly and take off the heat.
  6. 6FRY THE EGGS AND SERVE: in a separate pan, fry the eggs sunny-side up with crisp edges; divide the rice among bowls, top each with a fried egg, scatter over the seaweed strips and spring onion, then serve at once, breaking the yolk over the rice at the table.
💡
Tip: Salt the cabbage long enough that it bends without snapping — under-salted cabbage won't ferment cleanly.
