Chole Bhature

Chole bhature earned its popularity in the stalls and dhabas of Old Delhi and Punjab, where the pairing of tea-simmered chickpeas with pillowy deep-fried bread became a working-class breakfast built to fuel a long day of labor. Cooks in Amritsar and across Punjab are credited with darkening the chickpeas using black tea or dried amla, a trick that turned an everyday legume stew into something visually striking enough to anchor an entire stall. Today the dish is a fixture of Sunday brunch across North India, ladled onto steel plates alongside sliced raw onion, green chili, and a sharp pickle, with the bhature arriving hot and blistered straight from the oil. Its richness has made it something of an occasional indulgence rather than a daily meal, but few dishes announce a festive weekend morning in Delhi quite like a stack of golden bhature beside a bowl of dark, spiced chole.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500g dried chickpeas, soaked overnight
  • 2 black tea bags
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, pureed
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 tsp chana masala spice blend
  • 300g all-purpose flour
  • 100g plain yogurt
  • Oil, for deep-frying

How to make it

  1. 1Boil the overnight-soaked chickpeas with the tea bags and a pinch of salt until tender and deep brown in color.
  2. 2Drain the chickpeas, discard the tea bags, and set aside a little of the cooking liquid.
  3. 3Sauté the onions in oil until golden, then stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook until fragrant.
  4. 4Add the pureed tomatoes and the chana masala spice blend, letting the mixture simmer until the oil separates from the masala.
  5. 5Fold the cooked chickpeas into the pan along with a splash of the reserved liquid, then simmer until thick and glossy.
  6. 6Meanwhile, knead the flour with yogurt, a little oil, and salt into a firm dough, then let it rest for thirty minutes.
  7. 7Divide the dough, roll it into rounds, and deep-fry each piece until it puffs up golden, serving it hot alongside the chickpeas.
