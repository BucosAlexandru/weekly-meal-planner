Romanian Pan-Fried Meatballs

Chiftele are Romania's everyday pan-fried meat patties, easily confused by outsiders with mici, the country's famous skinless grilled sausages, but the two are unrelated in shape and technique. Where mici are rolled into short cylinders and cooked over an open grill without any binder, chiftele are flattened by hand into oval patties bound with bread soaked in milk, coated in breadcrumbs, and shallow-fried in a pan, a method closer to a European meatball or cutlet than to a grilled sausage. The dish is a fixture of everyday Romanian cooking rather than a festive or restaurant specialty, made from whatever ground meat is on hand and served hot with mashed potatoes or pickles, or cold the next day in a sandwich. Chiftele are also common picnic and travel food, since they hold up well at room temperature, and most Romanian households have their own small variation on the recipe, passed down rather than written.

Hand-shaped, flattened patties

The seasoned meat mixture is shaped into flat oval patties, distinct from the loose sausage shape of mici or the round balls of perișoare.

🍞

Bound with soaked bread

Bread soaked in milk, rather than raw rice, is worked into the mixture, keeping the patties tender and moist inside.

🌾

Coated in breadcrumbs

Each patty is rolled in breadcrumbs before frying, forming a crisp golden crust that seals in the juices.

🍳

Pan-fried, not grilled

Chiftele are shallow-fried in oil on the stovetop, unlike mici, which are always cooked over an open grill.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500g ground pork (or a pork-beef mix)
  • 1 slice stale white bread, soaked in milk
  • 1 egg
  • 1 small onion, grated
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
  • 100g breadcrumbs, for coating
  • Sunflower oil, for frying
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste

How to make it

  1. 1Squeeze the excess milk out of the soaked bread, then mash it with a fork until smooth.
  2. 2Combine the ground meat with the mashed bread, egg, grated onion, minced garlic, parsley, salt and pepper in a large bowl, mixing well by hand.
  3. 3Shape the mixture into flattened oval patties about the size of your palm, slightly thicker than a burger.
  4. 4Coat each patty evenly in breadcrumbs, pressing gently so the crumbs stick.
  5. 5Heat a generous layer of sunflower oil in a large frying pan over medium heat.
  6. 6Fry the chiftele in batches for several minutes on each side, until deeply golden brown and cooked through.
  7. 7Drain the chiftele briefly on paper towels and serve hot or at room temperature, with mashed potatoes or a simple salad.
