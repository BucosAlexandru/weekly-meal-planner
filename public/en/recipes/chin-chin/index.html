Chin Chin

Chin Chin is Nigeria's ubiquitous crunchy snack, present at every party, wedding, baptism, or Christmas celebration, often sold in large bags roadside by street vendors. The exact origin of the name remains unclear, with some tracing it to an onomatopoeia of the crunching sound made while chewing, others to older linguistic influences in the region. In Nigerian homes, a container full of Chin Chin is a sign of hospitality — guests are always offered a handful of crunchy pieces alongside a cold drink, and large, airtight tins are reserved exclusively for this snack in most kitchens. While the basic recipe stays simple, regional variations are numerous: some families add grated orange zest, others prefer a harder, almost biscuit-like texture, cooked at lower heat for longer.

🥛

Nutmeg and vanilla aroma

A little grated nutmeg and vanilla extract go into the dough, giving the snack a warm, instantly recognizable aroma.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500 g flour
  • 150 g cold butter, cubed
  • 120 g sugar
  • 2 eggs
  • 1/2 tsp grated nutmeg
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • A pinch of salt
  • Oil for frying

How to make it

  1. 1The flour is mixed with the baking powder, salt, and grated nutmeg in a large bowl, forming the dry base of the dough.
  2. 2The cold cubed butter is quickly rubbed into the flour mixture with fingertips until the mixture takes on the texture of damp breadcrumbs, with no large chunks of butter visible.
  3. 3Sugar is evenly incorporated, then eggs beaten with vanilla extract are gradually added, gently kneading a compact dough that doesn't stick to hands.
  4. 4The dough rests covered for fifteen minutes, then is divided into smaller pieces to make it easier to handle.
  5. 5Each piece is rolled out with a rolling pin into a thick layer about half a centimeter, on a surface dusted with a little flour.
  6. 6The rolled sheet is cut with a sharp knife or a toothed roller into strips, then into small, uniform cubes about two centimeters across.
  7. 7The cubes fry in small batches, in moderately hot oil, for a few minutes, until evenly browned on all sides, stirring constantly with a slotted spoon.
  8. 8The fried Chin Chin is lifted onto paper towels to remove excess oil, then left to cool completely before being stored in an airtight jar.
