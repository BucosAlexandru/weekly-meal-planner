Cochinita Pibil

Cochinita pibil comes from the Yucatán Peninsula, where the Maya cooked meat in underground pits called pib long before pork itself arrived with the Spanish; the technique survived even as the ingredient changed. Achiote, the rust-colored seed of the annatto tree, gives the dish both its signature color and a faintly peppery, earthy flavor, while sour orange, native to the region, supplies the acidity that tenderizes the meat during its long marinade. Traditionally the wrapped pork was lowered into a stone-lined pit filled with hot coals and left to cook for hours beneath the earth, a method still used for Sunday feasts in Yucatán villages. Modern cooks recreate the same slow, banana-leaf-wrapped result in an oven or a pot, and the dish now travels far beyond its home region, showing up in taco stands and restaurants across Mexico and increasingly abroad.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 2 kg pork shoulder, cut into large chunks
  • 100g achiote paste (annatto seed paste)
  • 1 cup sour orange juice (or mix of orange and lime juice)
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Banana leaves, for wrapping
  • 1 red onion, thinly sliced, for pickled onion garnish

How to make it

  1. 1Blend the achiote paste with sour orange juice, garlic, oregano, cumin, salt, and pepper into a thick, brick-red marinade.
  2. 2Rub the pork chunks generously with the marinade and let them rest, covered, in the refrigerator for at least four hours or overnight.
  3. 3Pass banana leaves briefly over an open flame to soften them, then line a deep baking dish or pot with the leaves.
  4. 4Place the marinated pork in the center and fold the leaves tightly over the meat to seal in the juices.
  5. 5Cook the wrapped pork low and slow, either in a low oven or a covered pot, for about three to four hours until it falls apart easily.
  6. 6While the pork cooks, toss the sliced red onion with lime juice and a pinch of salt so it turns bright pink and tangy.
  7. 7Unwrap the tender pork, shred it with two forks, and serve it piled onto warm tortillas with the pickled onion on top.
