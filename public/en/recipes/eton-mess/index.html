Eton Mess

Eton Mess is a simple yet beloved English dessert made of strawberries, whipped cream and crushed meringue, casually folded together into a deliberately messy look — hence the name. Tradition holds that the dessert originated at the prestigious Eton College, initially served at the annual cricket matches between Eton and Harrow, an important social event on the British school calendar. A popular story, though historically unconfirmed, claims the dessert was created by accident after a meringue or cream pie was dropped or accidentally crushed, and the messy result turned out so delicious it became intentional. Its simplicity — no cooking, just assembly — has made it a popular summer dessert across Britain, celebrated for the contrast between the crisp meringue and the velvety cream.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500 g fresh strawberries
  • 3 tbsp icing sugar
  • 1 tsp lemon juice
  • 300 ml double cream
  • 150 g baked meringues, bought or homemade
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1 tbsp caster sugar for the cream
  • A few mint leaves to garnish
  • A pinch of salt

How to make it

  1. 1Wash and hull the strawberries, then cut into small pieces, keeping a few whole for garnish.
  2. 2Place half the strawberries in a bowl with icing sugar and lemon juice and lightly crush them with a fork, leaving a chunky texture.
  3. 3Whip the cream with caster sugar and vanilla extract until soft peaks form, without over-whipping it.
  4. 4Crumble the meringues by hand into uneven pieces, some larger, some smaller, for varied texture.
  5. 5Add the crushed strawberries over the whipped cream and fold gently, only a few times, without fully combining.
  6. 6Fold in the crumbled meringue and the remaining chopped strawberries, keeping the characteristic messy look.
  7. 7Divide into individual bowls, garnish with mint leaves and serve immediately, while the meringue is still crisp.
