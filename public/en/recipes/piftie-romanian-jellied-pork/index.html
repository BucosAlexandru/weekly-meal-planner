Piftie (Romanian Jellied Pork)

Piftie is the dish that appears on the Romanian table almost exclusively in winter, especially at Christmas and New Year, when the cold outside keeps the jelly firm with no extra effort. The name comes from Slavic, by way of the Balkans, but the underlying technique — slowly simmering collagen-rich bones until the broth turns into a natural gel — is shared by many kitchens across the region, each with its own preferred cuts and seasonings. What separates a successful piftie from a failed one is that very collagen: without enough pork knuckles or trotters in the pot, the broth stays liquid no matter how long it sits in the fridge, since the traditional recipe has no gelatin to add afterward. The garlic, in turn, is always stirred in at the end, into the warm broth, never boiled — if it simmered alongside the meat for hours, it would lose its strength and leave only a muted trace instead of the sharp bite that balances the richness of the piftie.

Ingredients 6 servings

  • 1.2kg pork knuckles (trotters and hock)
  • 1 carrot
  • 1 parsnip root
  • 1 whole onion, unpeeled
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tsp peppercorns
  • 6 garlic cloves, crushed
  • Salt, to taste
  • Vinegar, to serve

How to make it

  1. 1PREPARE THE MEAT: put the pork knuckles in a large pot, cover with cold water and bring to a boil over medium heat.
  2. 2SKIM: constantly remove the grey foam from the surface for the first 15-20 minutes, until the broth turns clear.
  3. 3SIMMER SLOWLY: lower the heat, add the carrot, parsnip, whole onion, bay leaves and peppercorns.
  4. 4Simmer without boiling, partly covered, for 3-3.5 hours, until the meat pulls easily from the bone.
  5. 5SEASON: add salt near the end of cooking, tasting the broth — it should be slightly saltier than usual, since the flavour mellows once chilled.
  6. 6STRAIN AND SEPARATE: remove the meat from the broth, debone it and cut it into small pieces, discarding the thick skin and large cartilage.
  7. 7Strain the broth through a fine sieve.
  8. 8ADD THE GARLIC: stir the crushed garlic straight into the warm broth without boiling it again — the residual heat is enough to soften its bite without losing its strength.
  9. 9ASSEMBLE: divide the meat pieces between bowls or individual moulds, then pour the warm broth over them until fully covered.
  10. 10CHILL AND SERVE: let cool at room temperature briefly, then refrigerate for at least 6 hours or overnight, until the broth sets into a firm gel.
  11. 11Serve cold, straight from the mould or unmoulded onto a plate, with vinegar and fresh bread.
