Turkish Mantı

Mantı is considered a test of a cook's patience and skill in central Anatolia, especially around the city of Kayseri, where a family's reputation could literally be measured by the size of the dough squares — the smaller, the more appreciated the dish. Stories say some grandmothers claimed 40 pieces of mantı should fit on a single spoon, a standard almost impossible to reach today, but one that shows how much miniaturization matters in local tradition. The open boat shape, instead of a fully sealed closure like other dumplings, isn't just decorative: it lets the boiling liquid soak directly into the ground meat inside, flavoring it during cooking, not just at the end. The final contrast between the warm dough, the cold garlic yogurt and the hot butter poured right before serving creates three distinct temperature layers in a single bowl — a technical detail that disappears completely if the mantı are left to cool before being assembled.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 2 cups flour
  • 1 egg
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • Water, as needed for the dough
  • 250g ground beef or lamb
  • 1 small onion, grated
  • 500g thick yogurt
  • 3 garlic cloves, crushed
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 1 tsp sweet or hot paprika, or dried mint

How to make it

  1. 1KNEAD THE DOUGH: mix the flour, egg, salt and enough water, knead until you get an elastic, firm dough, let it rest covered 30 minutes.
  2. 2PREPARE THE FILLING: mix the ground meat with the grated onion, salt and pepper.
  3. 3ROLL AND CUT: roll the dough very thin, cut into small squares, 2-3cm.
  4. 4FILL AND SHAPE: place a little filling in the center of each square, lift the corners and pinch them at the top, forming an open shape.
  5. 5BOIL: boil the mantı in beef broth or boiling salted water, 8-10 minutes, until they float to the surface and the dough is tender.
  6. 6PREPARE THE SAUCE: mix the yogurt with the crushed garlic and a little salt.
  7. 7ASSEMBLE: drain the mantı, place them in a bowl, pour the garlic yogurt over.
  8. 8MELT THE BUTTER: melt the butter with the paprika or mint, until colored, pour it hot over the yogurt, right before serving.
