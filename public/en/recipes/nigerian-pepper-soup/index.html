Nigerian Pepper Soup

Nigerian pepper soup, known simply as "pepper soup," is considered an essential dish in Nigerian cuisine, valued both as comfort food and as a traditional remedy for colds, fever, or the postnatal period, thanks to the intense warming effect produced by the spice blend. The "pepper soup spice" mix, which includes dried calabash nutmeg seeds (locally called ehuru or uda), is considered the key to the dish's authenticity. Pepper soup varies significantly depending on the type of meat used — beef, goat, fish, or even offal such as beef tripe (shaki) — each version having devoted supporters. In many Nigerian families, serving a hot pepper soup is the first gesture of hospitality offered to a guest arriving tired or cold, and its intense heat is considered nearly therapeutic.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 800g beef or goat meat, cut into bone-in pieces
  • 1.5 liters water
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 tbsp pepper soup spice blend
  • 2-3 scotch bonnet chilies, crushed
  • 1 bouillon cube
  • Salt to taste
  • A bunch of uziza or basil leaves, chopped

How to make it

  1. 1PREPARE THE MEAT: Place the bone-in pieces of meat in a large pot, add cold water, bring to a boil and skim off the foam.
  2. 2ADD THE AROMATICS: Add the onion, garlic, ginger, crushed chilies and bouillon cube to the pot.
  3. 3SIMMER: Let the meat simmer over low heat 1-1.5 hours, until tender, adding water if needed.
  4. 4ADD THE SPICES: Add the pepper soup spice blend, stirring well so it dissolves into the broth.
  5. 5CONTINUE SIMMERING: Let the soup simmer another 15-20 minutes, over low heat, so the spices release their full flavor.
  6. 6SEASON: Taste and adjust the salt and heat, adding more chili if you prefer it spicier.
  7. 7ADD THE LEAVES: Add the chopped leaves in the last 2-3 minutes of cooking, so they keep their freshness.
  8. 8SERVE: Serve hot, in deep bowls, as a standalone soup or alongside boiled rice or yam.
