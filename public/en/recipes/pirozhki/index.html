Pirozhki

Pirozhki emerged in medieval Russian cuisine as a practical way to use small amounts of leftover meat, vegetables, or eggs, conveniently packaged in a portable dough, easy to carry to field work. The name derives from the Russian word 'pir', meaning feast or celebration, reflecting the dish's origin as festive food before becoming an everyday snack. In Tsarist Russia, pirozhki were sold on the streets by vendors, and each region developed its own preferred fillings — those from Siberia often included fish, while those from the south used more cabbage and mushrooms. Today, pirozhki remain a popular street food across all countries of the former Soviet Union, sold at metro and bus stations, considered a quick, cheap, and always satisfying snack.

Fluffy yeasted dough

The yeasted dough rises twice, giving the pirozhki a light, fluffy texture, unlike the denser dough used for other pastries.

🥩

Ground meat and onion filling

Ground beef or pork mixes with sautéed onion and fresh herbs, forming the classic savory filling.

🥚

Egg and dill variant

A popular alternative filling combines chopped hard-boiled eggs with fresh dill and green onion, suitable for the vegetarian version.

🍳

Fried in oil or baked in the oven

Pirozhki are traditionally fried in hot oil for a golden, crispy crust, though the oven-baked version is becoming increasingly popular.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500 g flour
  • 7 g dry yeast
  • 250 ml lukewarm milk
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 400 g ground beef
  • 2 onions, finely chopped

How to make it

  1. 1The yeast is dissolved in the lukewarm milk with the sugar, left for ten minutes until it foams.
  2. 2The flour is mixed with salt in a large bowl, then the yeast mixture, egg, and oil are added, kneading an elastic dough for ten minutes.
  3. 3The dough is covered and left to rise in a warm spot for an hour, until it doubles in volume.
  4. 4The finely chopped onion sautés in oil until translucent and lightly golden, then left to cool.
  5. 5The ground meat mixes with the sautéed onion, salt, and pepper, forming the dense, flavorful filling.
  6. 6The risen dough is divided into equal pieces, each rolled out with a rolling pin into a thin disc.
  7. 7A spoonful of filling is placed in the center of each disc, and the edges are folded and pressed firmly together, forming a sealed oval shape.
  8. 8The shaped pirozhki are left to rise a further ten minutes, then fried in hot oil over medium heat, three to four minutes per side, until golden and crispy.
  9. 9The pirozhki are lifted onto paper towels to remove excess oil and served warm, often alongside a light soup.
