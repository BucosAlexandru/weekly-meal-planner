Beef Wellington

Beef Wellington remains one of British cuisine's most persistent culinary mysteries: no solid evidence links the dish to Arthur Wellesley, the first Duke of Wellington, though legend has it he liked it so much the dish was named after him. Some culinary historians believe the name simply reflects the dish's cylindrical shape, resembling the Wellington boots worn by the duke. The written recipe doesn't appear until the mid-20th century in American cookbooks, which makes the historical connection genuinely uncertain. The critical technique is managing moisture: the seared meat must be fully cooled before being wrapped, and the duxelles must be cooked until completely dry, or steam released during baking will soften the pastry from the inside, turning the crisp crust into a gluey layer. Professional chefs insist on repeated chilling between stages, precisely to keep the pastry cold enough to puff up and stay crisp when it hits the hot oven.

Beef fillet seared before wrapping

The meat is quickly browned over high heat on all sides, to seal in the juices before it goes into the pastry.

🍄

Mushroom duxelles, not plain mushrooms

The mushrooms are finely chopped and cooked until all the water evaporates, forming a dense paste that won't make the pastry soggy.

🥞

A thin pancake that traps moisture

A crêpe or a slice of Parma ham goes between the meat and the duxelles, as an extra barrier against moisture.

📦

Chilled in the fridge between stages

The roll goes back into the fridge after each layer, so the pastry stays cold and crisps properly in the oven.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 800g beef tenderloin, in one piece
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • Salt and pepper, generously
  • 2 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 500g button mushrooms, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 4-6 slices prosciutto or Parma ham
  • 500g puff pastry, store-bought or homemade
  • 2 egg yolks, beaten with 1 tbsp water, for brushing

How to make it

  1. 1SEAR THE MEAT: Heat the oil in a pan over high heat, brown the beef tenderloin on all sides, 1-2 minutes per side, then brush it with mustard and let it cool.
  2. 2MAKE THE DUXELLES: Finely chop the mushrooms in a food processor, then sauté them with the garlic in a dry pan over medium heat 10-12 minutes, stirring often, until all the water evaporates and a dense paste remains.
  3. 3LAYER IT UP: Spread plastic wrap on the counter, lay the prosciutto slices slightly overlapping, spread the duxelles over them, then place the cooled meat in the center.
  4. 4ROLL IT: Using the wrap as a guide, tightly roll the prosciutto and duxelles around the meat, forming a compact cylinder, then twist the ends of the wrap and refrigerate 20 minutes.
  5. 5WRAP IN PASTRY: Roll out the puff pastry, unwrap the meat cylinder and place it on top, then wrap it tightly, sealing the edges, and refrigerate again 20 minutes.
  6. 6BRUSH AND SCORE: Brush the pastry with beaten egg yolk, lightly score a decorative pattern on the surface, without cutting all the way through.
  7. 7BAKE: Bake at 200°C for 35-40 minutes, until the pastry turns golden and crisp; let it rest 10 minutes before cutting thick slices.
