Baklava

Baklava's layered construction of paper-thin filo and chopped nuts reflects a shared culinary heritage across the former Ottoman Empire, and Greece has long claimed its own distinct version built on walnuts rather than the pistachios more common further east. Ancient Greek bakers are credited by some food historians with the earliest layering techniques that eventually evolved into filo pastry, though the sweet in its familiar form took shape over centuries of exchange around the eastern Mediterranean. The contrast between the impossibly thin, crackling layers of dough and the dense, spiced nut filling is what gives the pastry its signature texture. Unlike Middle Eastern versions that are often soaked in rosewater or orange blossom syrup, Greek baklava is traditionally finished with a syrup built on honey, giving it a deeper, more resinous sweetness. The syrup must always be poured over freshly baked, still-hot pastry, since pouring cold syrup onto a hot pan or hot syrup onto a cooled one causes the delicate filo layers to turn soggy instead of staying crisp. Baklava is cut into diamonds before baking specifically so that the finished syrup can seep evenly into every piece, and it is traditionally served in small portions alongside strong Greek coffee.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 500g filo pastry sheets
  • 400g walnuts, finely chopped
  • 200g pistachios, finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 250g unsalted butter, melted
  • 400g sugar
  • 300ml water
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 2 tablespoons honey

How to make it

  1. 1Brush a large baking dish generously with melted butter to prepare it for layering.
  2. 2Combine the chopped walnuts, pistachios, and cinnamon in a bowl and set the nut mixture aside.
  3. 3Layer about ten sheets of filo in the dish, brushing each sheet lightly with melted butter before adding the next.
  4. 4Sprinkle a third of the nut mixture evenly over the buttered filo layers.
  5. 5Repeat the layering process twice more, alternating filo sheets and nut filling, then finish with a final stack of ten buttered filo sheets on top.
  6. 6Using a sharp knife, cut the assembled baklava into diamond shapes before baking, cutting all the way through to the bottom of the dish.
  7. 7Bake in a preheated oven at 160 degrees Celsius for about fifty minutes, until deeply golden and crisp.
  8. 8Meanwhile, simmer the sugar, water, lemon juice, and honey together for ten minutes to make a syrup, then pour the hot syrup evenly over the warm baklava as soon as it comes out of the oven.
