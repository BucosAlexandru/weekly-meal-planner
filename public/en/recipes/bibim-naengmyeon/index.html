Bibim Naengmyeon

Naengmyeon traces its roots to northern Korea, where harsh winters made cold buckwheat noodles surprisingly popular even in freezing weather — it's said that people in Pyongyang ate them in overheated rooms, so the noodles' chill would contrast with the hot air. The bibim version (mixed with a spicy sauce rather than served in broth) later became the favourite summer dish across the rest of South Korea, eaten mostly in the sweltering months, when a cold, tangy meal feels most fitting. What makes this dish special is the contrast of textures: buckwheat noodles, less stretchy than wheat ones, only get their characteristic bite if they're rinsed thoroughly of starch in ice water right after boiling. The sauce, with grated pear rather than just sugar, brings a natural sweetness that tempers the gochujang's heat without dulling it. The soft-boiled egg isn't just a garnish — its runny yolk, mixed into the sauce, rounds out the whole flavour with every bite.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 350 g dried buckwheat noodles (naengmyeon)
  • 1 Asian pear, peeled, half grated and half thinly sliced
  • 4 tablespoons gochujang
  • 2 tablespoons gochugaru
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons rice vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 cucumber, thinly sliced
  • 100 g red cabbage, thinly shredded
  • 1 carrot, thinly sliced
  • 4 soft-boiled eggs (6 minutes)
  • 1 tablespoon toasted sesame seeds

How to make it

  1. 1MAKE THE SAUCE: purée half the pear until smooth, then stir it together with the gochujang, gochugaru, sugar, soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil and garlic — the sauce should be thick, glossy and balanced between sweet, sour and spicy.
  2. 2BOIL THE NOODLES: cook the buckwheat noodles in boiling water for 3-4 minutes, until al dente — don't let them go soft, buckwheat cooks fast.
  3. 3SHOCK THEM COLD: drain the noodles and transfer them straight into a large bowl of ice water, rubbing gently with your hands to wash off the surface starch; this gives them their characteristic springy bite.
  4. 4DRAIN WELL: lift the noodles out of the ice water and drain thoroughly, shaking off the excess, then divide among deep bowls.
  5. 5ADD THE SAUCE AND VEGETABLES: spoon 2-3 tablespoons of sauce over each portion and toss the noodles gently with the sauce using chopsticks or tongs, then arrange the thinly sliced cucumber, red cabbage and carrot on top.
  6. 6FINISH AND SERVE: halve each soft-boiled egg and set it over the noodles, scatter with toasted sesame seeds and serve at once, while the noodles are still cold and the sauce fresh.
