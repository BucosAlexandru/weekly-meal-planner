Romanian Eggplant Salad

Romanian eggplant salad is a home-kitchen recipe passed down by eye rather than exact measurement — every grandmother has her own oil ratio and her own trick for avoiding bitterness. The dish depends entirely on one technique: charring the eggplants directly over a flame rather than in the oven, for the smoke that penetrates deep into the flesh and gives the specific flavour that plain baking can't reproduce. What separates a successful salad from a mediocre one is patience at two stages: fully draining the bitter liquid from the flesh, without which the texture stays watery, and working in the oil in a thin stream, gradually, as with a mayonnaise. Added too quickly, the oil separates and the salad turns greasy instead of taking on the creamy, slightly airy consistency that defines it.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 2 large eggplants (600-700g)
  • 1 small onion, very finely chopped
  • 100ml sunflower oil
  • Salt, to taste
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Tomatoes and olives, to garnish

How to make it

  1. 1CHAR THE EGGPLANTS: put the whole eggplants directly over a gas flame or on a grill and cook for 15-20 minutes, turning often, until the skin is completely blackened and the flesh has softened.
  2. 2LET THEM STEAM: put the hot eggplants in a bag or covered dish for 10 minutes — the steam helps loosen the skin.
  3. 3PEEL AND DRAIN: remove the charred skin, then put the flesh in a sieve and let it drain for 15-20 minutes to lose the bitter liquid.
  4. 4CHOP BY HAND: chop the drained flesh finely with a knife on a wooden board until you get a chunky texture, not a purée.
  5. 5STIR IN THE ONION: add the finely chopped onion to the eggplant and mix.
  6. 6ADD THE OIL GRADUALLY: pour in the oil in a thin stream, stirring constantly with a fork or wooden spoon, until the mixture binds together and turns creamy.
  7. 7SEASON AND SERVE: add salt and lemon juice to taste, stir again and serve at room temperature, garnished with tomatoes and olives.
