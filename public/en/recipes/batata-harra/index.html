Batata Harra

Batata harra emerged from Lebanon's mezze culture as a way to give a humble vegetable the same bold, punchy treatment as meat or seafood dishes, and it owes its name simply to the fact that it is, quite literally, hot: hara means spicy in Arabic, distinguishing it from countless other potato preparations across the Arab world that carry no heat at all. Coastal cities like Beirut and Tripoli are often credited with popularizing the version built on frying rather than roasting, a technique that spread inland as home cooks looked for a faster route to the same crisp exterior. What keeps batata harra from tasting like plain fried potatoes with garlic is timing: the garlic, chili, and coriander are added only in the final seconds so their flavor stays sharp and fresh rather than mellowing into the oil, a habit that separates a good version from a merely adequate one. It rarely appears alone on a Lebanese table, showing up instead as one plate among many in a mezze spread that might include hummus, tabbouleh, and grilled meats, meant to be picked at slowly rather than eaten as a single-dish meal.

🌶️

Heat from fresh chili, not just pepper

Batata harra means "hot potatoes" in Arabic, and the kick comes from fresh red chili or chili flakes fried into the garlic rather than from black pepper alone.

🧄

A garlic and coriander finish, added last

Chopped garlic and fresh coriander are stirred in only at the very end of cooking, kept just long enough to turn fragrant without burning or turning bitter.

🍋

A squeeze of lemon right before serving

A final hit of lemon juice cuts through the oil and heat, which is why the dish is almost always finished at the table rather than in the kitchen.

🍽️

A mezze regular, not a side reserved for dinner

Far from a plain side dish, batata harra is a fixture of the Lebanese mezze spread, served alongside hummus and other small plates rather than next to a main course.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 1 kg potatoes, peeled and cut into 2 cm cubes
  • Vegetable oil, for frying
  • 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1-2 fresh red chilies, finely chopped, or 1 tsp chili flakes
  • 1/2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 cup fresh coriander (cilantro) leaves, chopped
  • 1 lemon, juiced
  • 1 tsp salt, or to taste
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper

How to make it

  1. 1Pat the potato cubes dry with a clean towel, since removing surface moisture is what keeps them from steaming instead of crisping in the oil.
  2. 2Heat about 3 cm of vegetable oil in a deep pan or pot until it reaches around 175°C, hot enough that a cube dropped in sizzles immediately.
  3. 3Fry the potatoes in batches for 6-8 minutes each, turning occasionally, until deeply golden and crisp on every side, then lift them out onto a paper-towel-lined plate.
  4. 4Season the hot potatoes immediately with salt while the oil still clings to them, which helps the seasoning stick.
  5. 5In a small pan, warm 2 tablespoons of the frying oil over medium heat and add the garlic, chili, and ground coriander, stirring for about 30 seconds until fragrant but not browned.
  6. 6Toss the fried potatoes with this garlic-chili oil, making sure every piece gets coated.
  7. 7Finish with the chopped fresh coriander, a generous squeeze of lemon juice, and a crack of black pepper, then serve immediately while still hot and crisp.
