Bò Kho

Bò kho is considered one of the few Vietnamese recipes that visibly carries the imprint of French cuisine, picked up during the colonial period. The basic structure — beef braised for a long time with carrots, in a thick sauce — recalls French daube-style stews or bœuf bourguignon, but the spices are entirely Vietnamese: lemongrass, star anise and cinnamon turn the dish into something quite different from its European root. Unlike many stews served only for lunch or dinner, bò kho is primarily a breakfast dish in southern Vietnam, eaten with crusty bread (bánh mì) dipped in the sauce or with rice noodles. The key to the technique is dry-toasting the whole spices before they go into the pot — a simple step, but one that releases their aromatic oils and gives the sauce a depth that adding them raw never could.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 800g beef shank or chuck, cut into large chunks
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, smashed and cut into pieces
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced, divided
  • 3 whole star anise
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 3 carrots, cut into large chunks
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tbsp annatto oil (or tomato paste)
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 700ml beef stock or water
  • Crusty bread or rice noodles, for serving
  • Thai basil and fresh cilantro, for serving

How to make it

  1. 1MARINATE THE BEEF: mix the beef chunks with the lemongrass, half the garlic and the fish sauce, refrigerate at least 1 hour.
  2. 2TOAST THE SPICES: dry-toast the star anise and cinnamon stick in a pot for a few minutes, until fragrant.
  3. 3SEAR THE BEEF: heat the annatto oil in the same pot, brown the marinated beef in batches, a few minutes per side.
  4. 4ADD THE ONION AND LIQUID: add the onion and remaining garlic, sauté briefly, then pour in the beef stock, bring to a boil.
  5. 5SIMMER: cover and simmer on low for 1.5-2 hours, until the beef is tender.
  6. 6ADD THE CARROTS: add the carrot chunks in the last 20-30 minutes of cooking.
  7. 7ADJUST THE SEASONING: add sugar and fish sauce to taste.
  8. 8SERVE: serve hot with crusty bread or rice noodles, topped with Thai basil and cilantro.
