Rakott Krumpli

Rakott krumpli is the everyday Hungarian layered potato bake, built from ingredients nearly every household kept on hand: potatoes, eggs, smoked sausage, and sour cream. It belongs to a broader family of Central European "rakott" dishes, a word that simply means "layered" or "stacked," and it likely grew out of the practical habit of stretching a handful of pantry staples into a filling one-dish meal for a large family. Because it required no special equipment beyond an oven dish, rakott krumpli became a staple of Hungarian Sunday lunches and school cafeterias alike, prized for reheating just as well the next day. Regional and family variations abound — some cooks add a layer of grated cheese, others skip the sausage entirely for a meatless version during fasting periods — but the core stack of potato, egg, and sour cream remains constant across the country.

Baked and cut into squares

The whole dish bakes until bubbling, then rests briefly before being cut into hearty squares.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 1kg potatoes, boiled and sliced
  • 6 hard-boiled eggs, sliced
  • 300g smoked Hungarian sausage, sliced
  • 400ml sour cream
  • 60g butter, melted
  • 2 tbsp breadcrumbs
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • Salt and black pepper
  • Fresh dill, chopped, for garnish

How to make it

  1. 1Boil the potatoes whole in their skins until just tender, then peel and slice them into thick rounds once cool enough to handle.
  2. 2Boil the eggs until hard, cool them under running water, peel, and slice them into rounds as well.
  3. 3Grease a deep baking dish with butter and scatter breadcrumbs over the bottom to keep the base from sticking.
  4. 4Layer a third of the potato slices in the dish, season lightly with salt and pepper, then add a layer of sliced eggs and a layer of sausage.
  5. 5Spoon a generous layer of sour cream over the top, sprinkle with a little paprika, and repeat the layering twice more, finishing with potatoes and a final coat of sour cream.
  6. 6Drizzle the melted butter over the top and scatter the remaining breadcrumbs to encourage a golden crust, then bake until bubbling and lightly browned.
  7. 7Let the casserole rest for ten minutes before cutting into squares, scattering with fresh dill just before serving.
