Hot and Sour Soup

Hot and sour soup is a northern Chinese classic, most associated with Beijing and Sichuan tables, built on a bracing contrast: the sourness of black vinegar against a generous, almost fierce hit of white pepper. Silky ribbons of egg, soft tofu and earthy dried mushrooms and wood ear give it body and a satisfying tangle of textures. Unlike chilli-driven dishes, the heat here is peppery rather than fiery — a warming sharpness that clears the head. The two make-or-break moments are the seasoning, which should taste bold and balanced before you thicken, and the egg, poured in a thin stream off the heat so it sets into fine threads rather than clumping. Adjust the vinegar and pepper at the very end, when their aromas are brightest.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 1.2 l unsalted chicken stock (or vegetable stock for a meat-free version)
  • 150 g firm tofu, cut into thin strips about 5 cm long
  • 6 dried shiitake mushrooms, soaked in hot water 20 min and sliced (keep the soaking water)
  • 10 g dried wood ear mushrooms, soaked and shredded
  • 80 g bamboo shoots, cut into thin strips
  • 100 g pork loin, cut into thin strips (optional — omit for vegetarian)
  • 3 tbsp Chinese black vinegar (Chinkiang), plus more to taste
  • 1.5 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 1 tsp dark soy sauce (for colour)
  • 1.5 tsp white pepper — the soup should taste distinctly peppery
  • 0.5 tsp sugar
  • 2 tbsp cornstarch (cornflour) mixed with 4 tbsp cold water
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 2 spring onions, finely sliced
  • 0.5 tsp salt, to taste

How to make it

  1. 1PREP: soak the shiitake and wood ear mushrooms in hot water until soft, then slice the shiitake and shred the wood ear; slice the tofu, bamboo shoots and pork into matching thin strips so everything cooks evenly.
  2. 2SIMMER: bring the stock to a gentle simmer (add the strained shiitake soaking water for extra depth); add the pork, mushrooms and bamboo shoots and simmer 5 minutes, skimming any foam.
  3. 3ADD TOFU: slide in the tofu strips and simmer 2 minutes, stirring gently so they do not break.
  4. 4SEASON: stir in the light and dark soy, sugar, white pepper and 3 tbsp black vinegar; taste — it should be assertively peppery and sharply sour, so add more pepper or vinegar until it sings.
  5. 5THICKEN: stir the cornstarch slurry again and pour it in slowly while stirring; simmer 1 minute until the soup turns silky and lightly thickened, enough to coat a spoon.
  6. 6EGG RIBBONS: turn off the heat; hold a fork over the surface and pour the beaten egg in a thin, steady stream while stirring the soup in a slow circle — the egg sets into delicate ribbons.
  7. 7FINISH: stir in the sesame oil, scatter the spring onion, and add a final splash of vinegar off the heat so its aroma stays bright; serve piping hot.
