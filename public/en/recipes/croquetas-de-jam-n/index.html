Croquetas de Jamón

Croquetas de Jamón arrived in Cuba with waves of Spanish immigrants, especially from Galicia and Asturias, who brought with them the technique of cooking with a thick béchamel from northern Spain. On the island, the dish became an essential home staple, served as a snack at any hour, at parties, or as an appetizer before the main meal, adapted with locally available ham instead of jamón serrano. What separates a good croquette from a mediocre one is discipline at two stages: the consistency of the béchamel, much thicker than a regular sauce, and chilling it long enough before shaping. Without at least 2 hours of chilling, the mixture is too soft to hold its shape while breading, and the croquettes would burst open in the oil. The double breading layer — flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs — creates a watertight barrier, letting the creamy interior stay intact while the outside fries quickly and turns crisp.

Far more flour and less milk than a regular béchamel, so the mixture sets firm when chilled

❄️

The mixture chills in the fridge for at least 2 hours before shaping

Without this chilling, the croquettes would fall apart while breading or frying

🍞

Each croquette goes through three stages: flour, egg, breadcrumbs

The double breading seals the creamy center completely, which would otherwise burst in the oil

🌡️

They fry briefly, in very hot oil

The inside is already cooked from the béchamel stage — frying only needs to brown the crust, not heat the center

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 4 tbsp butter
  • 4 tbsp flour
  • 500ml warm milk
  • 250g ham, very finely chopped
  • 1 small onion, very finely chopped
  • Salt, white pepper and nutmeg, to taste
  • 2 eggs, beaten
  • 150g fine breadcrumbs
  • Vegetable oil, for frying

How to make it

  1. 1SAUTÉ THE ONION: melt the butter in a saucepan, sauté the onion until soft, about 3 minutes.
  2. 2ADD THE FLOUR: sprinkle the flour over the onion, stir constantly for 2 minutes, to cook off the raw taste.
  3. 3POUR IN THE MILK: pour in the warm milk gradually, stirring constantly so no lumps form, until you get a very thick sauce.
  4. 4ADD THE HAM: stir in the chopped ham, season with salt, white pepper and nutmeg, cook 2-3 more minutes.
  5. 5CHILL: transfer the mixture to a tray, spread it evenly, cover with plastic wrap pressed directly onto the surface and refrigerate at least 2 hours, ideally overnight.
  6. 6SHAPE AND BREAD: form small cylinders with damp hands, roll them through flour, then beaten egg, then breadcrumbs.
  7. 7FRY: heat the oil to 180°C, fry the croquettes in small batches, 2-3 minutes, until golden.
  8. 8SERVE: drain on paper towels and serve hot.
