Drob de Miel (Romanian Lamb Pâté)

Drob is the unavoidable dish of the Romanian Easter table, made specifically from the offal of the lamb slaughtered for the holiday meal — a recipe born from the custom of wasting nothing from a whole animal. Its structure, with a row of whole eggs running through the middle, carries its own Easter symbolism, and slicing it reveals the yellow ring like a small sun at the centre of each piece. What separates a successful drob from a mediocre one comes down to two things: briefly boiling the offal before chopping it, just enough to cook it through without turning it rubbery; and the thin membrane it's wrapped in — without it, the pâté crumbles when sliced, no matter how well the filling is bound on the inside. Serving it cold rather than warm isn't a simple preference: warm, the slices lose their shape and fall apart the moment they hit the plate.

Ingredients 8 servings

  • 600g lamb offal (liver, heart, lungs)
  • 300g lamb meat, minced
  • 150g lamb caul fat (or baking parchment, as a substitute)
  • 1 bunch dill, chopped
  • 1 bunch parsley, chopped
  • 3 spring onions, chopped
  • 4 eggs, hard-boiled and whole
  • 2 raw eggs
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Butter, for greasing the dish

How to make it

  1. 1BOIL THE OFFAL: put the lamb offal in a pot of salted water and boil for 15-20 minutes, until fully cooked but still firm.
  2. 2Drain and let it cool.
  3. 3CHOP FINELY: chop the boiled offal and the raw lamb meat finely, with a knife or through a mincer, until you get a fine, even texture.
  4. 4MIX THE FILLING: put the minced mixture in a large bowl, add the dill, parsley, spring onion, raw eggs, salt and pepper, and mix well by hand until everything binds together.
  5. 5PREPARE THE MOULD: butter a loaf tin and line the inside with the stretched-out caul fat, leaving enough hanging over the edges to fold over later.
  6. 6FILL HALFWAY: put half the mixture into the mould and level it with a spatula.
  7. 7LAY THE EGGS: place the whole boiled eggs in a row, end to end, along the centre of the mould.
  8. 8COVER COMPLETELY: add the rest of the mixture over the eggs, pressing gently so there are no gaps, then fold the caul fat over it, sealing completely.
  9. 9BAKE: bake at 180°C for 55-65 minutes, until nicely browned on top and a knife inserted in the centre comes out clean.
  10. 10COOL AND SERVE: let the pâté cool completely at room temperature, then refrigerate for at least 2 hours before slicing — it cuts much more cleanly cold than warm.
