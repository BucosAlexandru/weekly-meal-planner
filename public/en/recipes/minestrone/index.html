Minestrone

Minestrone literally means 'big soup' and is less a fixed recipe than a method — the classic dish of Italian peasant cooking, designed to use whatever vegetables were on hand and in season. There's no definitive ingredient list; every region, every home kitchen has its own version, and a cook in Liguria would use entirely different vegetables from one in Tuscany. What stays constant is the technique: vegetables diced small and sautéed at the start for depth, beans for body, and pasta added separately, never boiled in the big pot — otherwise it soaks up all the broth and the soup runs out before the last bowl is served. A parmesan rind tossed into the simmering pot is the old trick of Italian home kitchens: nothing gets wasted, and the rind leaves behind a depth you can't get any other way.

Ingredients 6 servings

  • 3 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 courgette, diced
  • 150 g green beans, cut into 2 cm pieces
  • 2 cans (400 g each) chopped tomatoes
  • 1.5 l vegetable stock
  • 1 parmesan rind (optional, but recommended)
  • 1 can (400 g) cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 sprig fresh thyme
  • 100 g small pasta (ditalini or short macaroni)
  • 150 g savoy cabbage or spinach, shredded
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Grated parmesan and fresh basil, to serve

How to make it

  1. 1SAUTÉ THE BASE VEGETABLES: heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat, add the onion, carrots and celery and cook 8-10 minutes, stirring often, until softened but not browned — this mixture is the flavour foundation of the soup.
  2. 2ADD THE GARLIC AND FIRMER VEGETABLES: stir in the garlic and cook 1 minute until fragrant, then add the courgette and green beans, stirring for 2-3 minutes.
  3. 3POUR IN THE LIQUIDS: add the chopped tomatoes, vegetable stock and the parmesan rind if using; stir well, scraping up anything stuck to the bottom of the pot.
  4. 4SIMMER WITH THE BEANS: add the cannellini beans, bay leaf and thyme, bring to a boil, then simmer gently, partially covered, for 25-30 minutes, until the vegetables are tender.
  5. 5MASH SOME OF THE BEANS: scoop out a few spoonfuls of the cannellini beans, mash them with a fork and stir them back into the pot — this thickens the broth naturally without any flour or cream.
  6. 6COOK THE PASTA SEPARATELY: in a separate pot of salted water, cook the small pasta until al dente, drain and set aside — cooking it directly in the soup means it soaks up all the broth and turns mushy before you finish serving the rest.
  7. 7ADD THE GREENS AND FINISH: remove the parmesan rind and bay leaf, stir in the shredded cabbage or spinach and simmer 3-4 minutes more until wilted; season with salt and pepper to taste.
  8. 8SERVE: spoon some cooked pasta into each bowl, ladle the hot soup over it, then scatter with grated parmesan and freshly chopped basil.
