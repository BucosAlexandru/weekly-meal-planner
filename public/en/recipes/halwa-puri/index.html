Halwa Puri

Halwa puri is Sunday breakfast in Pakistan par excellence, a weekly ritual so deeply rooted that many families cook nothing else on Sunday mornings — they buy their portion of halwa, puri and chana directly from specialized vendors who only appear on weekends. The combination of sweet semolina with salty fried bread and tamarind-spiced chickpeas reflects a logic of flavor balance inherited from Mughal cuisine, where sweet, salty and sour were meant to be served together for a complete meal. Traditional halwa puri vendors, known as "halwai," begin preparation hours before dawn, so the first batch is ready when the shop opens. The skill of frying puri so it puffs up fully without cracking is considered a hallmark of an experienced cook, and many customers watch this moment closely as part of the morning's culinary spectacle.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 1 cup fine semolina
  • 1/2 cup ghee
  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 3/4 cup sugar
  • A few strands of saffron
  • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
  • 2 tbsp chopped pistachios
  • 2 tbsp chopped almonds
  • 2 cups whole wheat flour
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • Water, as needed for the dough
  • Oil, for frying the puri

How to make it

  1. 1KNEAD THE PURI DOUGH: Mix the whole wheat flour with salt and a little oil, then gradually add water and knead into a firm, elastic dough.
  2. 2Cover and let it rest 20 minutes.
  3. 3PREPARE THE SYRUP: In a small saucepan, boil the water with the sugar, saffron and cardamom, until the sugar fully dissolves; keep the syrup warm.
  4. 4TOAST THE SEMOLINA: Melt the ghee in a thick-bottomed pan, add the semolina and toast it over medium-low heat, stirring continuously, until golden and nutty-smelling, 8-10 minutes.
  5. 5POUR IN THE SYRUP: Carefully pour the warm syrup over the semolina, stirring constantly — the mixture will sizzle and thicken quickly.
  6. 6COOK THE HALWA: Keep stirring over low heat until the halwa binds completely and pulls away from the sides of the pan, 3-4 minutes.
  7. 7ROLL OUT THE PURI: Divide the dough into small balls and roll each ball into a thin, round disc.
  8. 8HEAT THE OIL: Heat plenty of oil in a deep pan over medium-high heat.
  9. 9FRY THE PURI: Slip each dough disc into the hot oil and press gently with a slotted spoon — it will puff up like a balloon within seconds; remove it once golden on both sides.
  10. 10FINISH THE HALWA: Sprinkle the chopped pistachios and almonds over the warm halwa.
  11. 11SERVE: Serve the halwa immediately, alongside warm puri and, traditionally, spiced chickpeas (chana).
