Bableves

Bean soup has been peasant sustenance across the Hungarian countryside for centuries, prized because dried beans stored through the winter and could be stretched with whatever smoked meat, root vegetables, and a spoonful of paprika a household had on hand. The tiny pinched noodles called csipetke — torn straight from a scrap of dough between finger and thumb rather than rolled and cut — are a hallmark of many Hungarian soups and turn a simple bean broth into a filling, one-pot meal. In Catholic families, bableves without meat was traditionally served on Christmas Eve, when the fast still held, its heartiness making up for the missing pork. Today it remains one of Hungary's most common home-cooked soups, made in countless regional variations — some cooks thicken it with a paprika roux, others rely on the natural creaminess of well-cooked beans — but the combination of smoke, paprika, and a final swirl of sour cream stays constant.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 400g dried kidney beans, soaked overnight
  • 200g smoked pork knuckle or bacon, diced
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 1 parsley root or parsnip, diced
  • 2 tbsp sweet paprika
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 150g csipetke (pinched egg noodles)
  • 200ml sour cream and a splash of vinegar, to finish

How to make it

  1. 1Drain the beans that have soaked overnight, cover them with fresh water, and bring to a boil with the diced smoked pork.
  2. 2Skim off any foam that rises, then lower the heat and let the beans simmer gently for about an hour, until they start to soften.
  3. 3Add the chopped onion, diced carrot, and parsley root, and continue simmering until both the beans and vegetables are tender.
  4. 4In a small pan, warm a little oil, stir in the sweet paprika and minced garlic off the heat, then spoon this fragrant mixture into the pot to deepen the color and flavor.
  5. 5Meanwhile, make the csipetke dough from flour, egg, and a pinch of salt, then pinch off small irregular pieces with your fingers.
  6. 6Drop the pinched dough pieces directly into the simmering soup and cook until they float to the surface and are fully cooked through.
  7. 7Season with salt to taste, stir in a swirl of sour cream and a splash of vinegar just before serving, and ladle the soup hot into bowls.
