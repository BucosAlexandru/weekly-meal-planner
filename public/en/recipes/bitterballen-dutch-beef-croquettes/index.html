Bitterballen (Dutch Beef Croquettes)

Bitterballen are considered the most emblematic "bittergarnituur" — beer snack — of the Netherlands, apparently created in the nineteenth century as a way to use up leftover meat from larger ragouts, too little for a main course but enough to be turned into crispy croquettes. The name "bitterballen" refers to the custom of eating them alongside a glass of bitter — a traditional Dutch bitter liqueur — although today they're eaten just as often with beer. This snack has become a symbol of Dutch social "borrel" culture — an informal gathering, usually in the afternoon or evening, dedicated to drinks and conversation. No Dutch office gathering, party or festive event is considered complete without a tray of hot bitterballen, served with spicy mustard, a combination so widespread it's considered almost mandatory.

Beef ragout

A thick ragout of finely minced beef, slow-cooked with a roux, forms the croquette's core.

❄️

Chilled before shaping

The ragout is fully chilled, even refrigerated, so it can be shaped into compact balls.

🍞

Triple breaded

Each ball is coated in flour, beaten egg and breadcrumbs, often twice for an extra crispy layer.

🌭

With mustard

Served hot, alongside spicy Dutch mustard for dipping.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 400g beef, finely minced
  • 500ml beef broth
  • 60g butter
  • 60g flour
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1/2 tsp nutmeg
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • 100g flour (for breading)
  • 3 eggs, beaten
  • 200g breadcrumbs
  • Oil for frying
  • Dutch mustard, for serving

How to make it

  1. 1SAUTÉ THE ONION: Melt part of the butter in a saucepan, sauté the chopped onion 2-3 minutes, until translucent.
  2. 2ADD THE MEAT: Add the minced beef, cooking and stirring 5 minutes, until browned.
  3. 3MAKE THE ROUX: Add the rest of the butter, then sprinkle the flour over the meat, stirring constantly 2 minutes, to cook off the raw flour taste.
  4. 4POUR IN THE BROTH: Gradually add the beef broth, stirring continuously, until you get a thick, pudding-like consistency.
  5. 5SIMMER: Let the mixture simmer 10 minutes over low heat, stirring often, then add the parsley, nutmeg, salt and pepper.
  6. 6CHILL: Pour the mixture into a tray, spread it evenly, cover with plastic wrap pressed directly onto the surface, and refrigerate at least 4 hours, ideally overnight.
  7. 7SHAPE THE BALLS: Take portions of the chilled mixture with a wet spoon, shape them into compact balls the size of a golf ball.
  8. 8PREPARE THE BREADING: Place the flour, beaten eggs and breadcrumbs in three separate bowls.
  9. 9BREAD: Roll each ball in flour, then egg, then breadcrumbs; for an extra crispy layer, repeat the egg and breadcrumb steps.
  10. 10HEAT THE OIL: Heat the frying oil to 180°C.
  11. 11FRY: Fry the balls in batches, 3-4 minutes, until golden and crispy on the outside.
  12. 12SERVE: Remove onto paper towels, serve hot, with Dutch mustard on the side.
