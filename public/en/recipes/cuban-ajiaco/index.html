Cuban Ajiaco

Cuban ajiaco is considered by many the island's most representative dish, a culinary symbol as powerful as national identity itself — historian Fernando Ortiz famously used the image of an ajiaco pot to describe the mix of indigenous, African and Spanish cultures that formed modern Cuba. The original, pre-colonial recipe used only local root vegetables and animal meat; the arrival of pork and chicken with the Spanish, plus techniques brought by African slaves, gradually shaped it into today's rich version. Beyond the symbolism, the soup's technique lies in the careful sequencing of ingredients: each meat and each root vegetable has its own cooking time, and adding them in the wrong order would produce either dry meat or vegetables that fall apart completely. Malanga, unlike yuca or sweet potato, partially disintegrates during simmering, and it's exactly that released starch that turns a thin broth into a thick soup, close to the consistency of a stew.

Cuts through the richness built up by several meats, without masking the vegetables' flavor

The soup is always left to rest before serving

The root vegetables' starch binds into the broth, turning a thin liquid into a thick, near-stew soup

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 300g beef brisket, cut into chunks
  • 300g smoked or fresh pork ribs, cut into pieces
  • 1 chicken thigh, skin on
  • 2 liters water or beef broth
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 malanga (taro root), peeled and cubed
  • 1 large yuca, peeled and cut into pieces
  • 1 sweet potato, peeled and cut into pieces
  • 1 regular potato, peeled and cut into pieces
  • 1 green plantain, peeled and cut into pieces
  • 1 ear of corn, cut into rounds
  • Juice of 2 limes
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

How to make it

  1. 1BOIL THE BEEF: put the beef brisket in a large pot with water, bring to a boil, skim off the foam, then simmer 45 minutes.
  2. 2ADD THE REST OF THE MEAT: add the pork ribs and chicken thigh, continue simmering 30 minutes.
  3. 3SAUTÉ THE AROMATICS: in a separate pan, sauté the onion and garlic, add them to the pot.
  4. 4ADD THE FIRM ROOT VEGETABLES: add the malanga and yuca, the firmest vegetables, simmer 15 minutes.
  5. 5ADD THE REST OF THE VEGETABLES: add the sweet potato, regular potato, plantain and corn, simmer 20-25 more minutes, until all the vegetables are tender and the broth has thickened.
  6. 6SOUR AND SEASON: turn off the heat, add the lime juice, salt and pepper, taste and adjust.
  7. 7REST AND SERVE: let the soup rest 10 minutes before serving, so the flavors meld.
