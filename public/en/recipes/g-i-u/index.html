Gỏi Đu Đủ

Gỏi đu đủ belongs to a wide family of Vietnamese gỏi, salads built around a shredded raw vegetable tossed with a pounded, punchy dressing rather than a simple oil-based vinaigrette. Green papaya was an obvious choice for the technique: the unripe fruit is plentiful, cheap, and its firm flesh shreds into threads that hold their crunch even after sitting in dressing for a while, unlike leafier greens that would wilt. What defines a good gỏi đu đủ is less any single ingredient than the balance struck between them, since the dressing has to hit sour, salty, sweet, and spicy at once without letting any one flavor take over. Street vendors often make it to order, pounding the dressing fresh in a mortar for each portion, and toppings vary by region and vendor, from dried beef and shrimp to grilled pork or simply extra peanuts and herbs.

🥭

Green papaya shredded for crunch, not ripeness

Unripe papaya is used specifically because it's firm and starchy, shredded into long threads that stay crisp even after soaking in dressing.

🥜

A mortar and pestle brings it together

Garlic, chili, sugar, and lime are pounded together in a mortar rather than whisked, bruising the ingredients just enough to release their oils and juices.

⚖️

Balances sour, salty, sweet, and spicy at once

The dressing is tasted and adjusted constantly, aiming for a sharp equilibrium between fish sauce, lime, sugar, and chili rather than any single dominant flavor.

🥩

Dried beef and shrimp for contrast

Chewy dried beef and tender shrimp are layered over the shredded papaya, giving the crunchy salad pockets of savory protein in every bite.

Ingredients 4 servings

  • 400g green (unripe) papaya, shredded
  • 2 carrots, shredded
  • 150g cooked shrimp, halved
  • 100g dried beef jerky or grilled beef, thinly sliced
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce
  • 2 tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tbsp sugar
  • 2 cloves garlic and 2 red chilies, pounded
  • 50g roasted peanuts, crushed, and Vietnamese coriander leaves, for topping

How to make it

  1. 1Peel and shred the green papaya into long, thin threads, either with a special shredder or a coarse grater.
  2. 2Shred the carrots the same way and toss them together with the papaya in a large bowl.
  3. 3Pound the garlic and chilies in a mortar until roughly crushed, then add the sugar and pound briefly until it starts to dissolve.
  4. 4Stir in the fish sauce and lime juice, tasting and adjusting until the dressing is sharply sour, salty, and sweet all at once.
  5. 5Pour the dressing over the shredded papaya and carrots and toss well with your hands or tongs, letting the vegetables soften slightly.
  6. 6Add the shrimp and dried beef, tossing gently so they mix through without breaking down the shredded vegetables.
  7. 7Transfer the salad to a plate and top with crushed peanuts and Vietnamese coriander just before serving.
